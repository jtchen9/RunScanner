One-Week Mobility and Localization Experimental Program

# Title
One-Week Experimental Program for Robot Mobility, AprilTag Localization, and NMS Integration
Purpose: bring the robot mobility subsystem to a state suitable for structured data collection
Duration: nominally 7 days, 2–3 hours of lab work per day
Method: daily execution + same-day review + next-day revision
Priority structure:

    Must-have = required for milestone progress
    Further confirmation = only if results are borderline or time allows


=============

# 0. Program-level statement

## Motivation
The mobility subsystem is no longer at the stage of “can the robot move once.”
The real requirement is operational robustness:

the robot must receive commands from the NMS
execute them reliably
stop safely when required
report the outcome truthfully
support localization accurate enough for NMS route planning and restriction-zone enforcement
remain stable enough for extended operation before charging

The purpose of this one-week program is to turn the current partially validated mobility prototype into a system with known limits, known accuracy, and known operational procedures.


## Goal
At the end of the week, the team should be able to answer:

What AprilTag distances and angles are practically reliable?
How accurate is NMS localization using the tag data reported by the robot?
How repeatable are turning and forward movement commands?
What command ranges are operationally safe and useful?
Is the robot stable enough for repeated unattended command execution?
What restrictions or calibrations are still required before full data collection begins?


## Expectation
This program is expected to produce:
    a practical sensing envelope for AprilTag
    a practical localization accuracy bound for NMS
    calibration data for turning and forward movement
    a first robustness estimate for repeated command execution
    a clear decision on whether mobility is ready for the next phase

This program is not expected to finalize every detail.
It is expected to reach a point where the remaining unknowns are small, explicit, and manageable.



=============

# Day 1 — AprilTag Raw Measurement Characterization

## Overall motivation
Before calibrating movement or evaluating route execution, the team must know what the robot-side AprilTag outputs actually mean in practice. If the robot cannot sense tags reliably at operational distances and angles, then later localization and movement calibration will not be trustworthy.

## Overall goal
Determine the practical sensing envelope for AprilTag on the robot:
    detection success vs distance
    detection success vs horizontal angle
    repeatability of distance_m, angle_deg, and yaw_deg

## Overall expectation
At the end of Day 1, the team should know:
    usable frontal distance range
    usable horizontal angle range
    whether reported values are stable enough for NMS use
    whether extra tag deployment or different mounting assumptions are likely needed


====

## Day 1 Experiment A — Frontal Distance Sweep

### Priority
Must-have

### Motivation
The simplest sensing geometry is robot facing one tag directly. This experiment establishes the baseline performance before any more complex measurement is attempted.

### Goal
Measure AprilTag sensing at known frontal distances with angle approximately 0°.

### Expectation
Expected result:
    stable detection at short and medium range
    distance_m increasing monotonically with true distance
    angle_deg near 0°
    yaw_deg near 0°
If this experiment fails badly, most later experiments should be postponed.

====

## Day 1 Experiment B — Horizontal Angle Sweep

### Priority
Must-have

### Motivation
In real operation, the robot will often view tags from the left or right rather than directly head-on. The NMS needs to know what horizontal angle range remains reliable.

### Goal
Measure tag sensing at known left/right viewing angles at selected distances.

### Expectation
Expected result:
    detection strong near center
    degradation toward large angles
    correct sign and roughly correct magnitude of angle_deg
    less stable behavior at larger angles and longer range
If this experiment shows that useful tag sensing collapses outside a narrow angle range, then tag placement strategy must be revised before relying on mobility commands.


====

## Day 1 Experiment C — Further Confirmation Sweep

### Priority
Further confirmation

### Motivation
If Experiment A or B shows borderline behavior, extra intermediate measurements are needed to refine the practical operating boundary.

### Goal
Measure selected intermediate distances or angles to clarify the boundary between “reliable” and “unreliable.”

### Expectation
Expected result:
    smoother understanding of the practical sensing boundary
    confirmation of whether suspected problem regions are real or caused by setup inconsistency
This experiment should be skipped if Day 1 must-have results are already clear.

# Day 2 — NMS Localization Validation

## Overall motivation
Robot-side tag sensing is useful only if the NMS can convert the reported tag data into a sufficiently accurate robot position and heading estimate.

## Overall goal
Validate the full localization chain:
    robot sensing
    robot reporting
    NMS parsing
    NMS location calculation

## Overall expectation
At the end of Day 2, the team should know:
    typical position error
    typical heading error
    difference between one-tag and multi-tag conditions
    whether current tag placement is already sufficient for controlled mobility


====

## Day 2 Experiment A — Fixed-Point Localization Validation

### Priority
Must-have

### Motivation
The most direct way to validate NMS localization is to place the robot at known floor points with known headings and compare NMS output to truth.

### Goal
Measure NMS-estimated robot position and heading at fixed marked points.

### Expectation
Expected result:
    repeated measurements at the same point cluster reasonably tightly
    multi-tag points perform better than one-tag points
    heading estimate may be noisier than position but still useful
If errors are too large or too inconsistent, movement calibration should not be treated as final yet.


====

## Day 2 Experiment B — Weak-Zone Confirmation

### Priority
Further confirmation

### Motivation
If some fixed points appear much worse than others, extra measurements are needed to identify whether the problem comes from poor tag visibility, poor tag geometry, or unstable NMS inference.

### Goal
Re-measure only the weak or suspicious points with extra repetitions or additional headings.

### Expectation
Expected result:
    confirmation of whether the weak points are truly weak
    initial indication of where tag placement must be improved
Skip if Day 2 fixed-point results are already clean and consistent.


=============

# Day 3 — Turning Accuracy and Repeatability

## Overall motivation
Turning is already gyro-based and appears promising, but the mobility subsystem cannot be considered reliable until turn accuracy is characterized quantitatively.

## Overall goal
Measure the actual error and repeatability of turning commands across useful angles.

## Overall expectation
At the end of Day 3, the team should know:
    bias of turn execution
    repeatability across repeated turns
    whether turn commands are good enough as-is
    whether NMS should compensate residual heading error


====

## Day 3 Experiment A — Signed Turn Validation

### Priority
Must-have

### Motivation
The new command interface uses a single mobility.turn command with positive angle for left and negative angle for right. This must be validated as a command interface and as a physical operation.

### Goal
Measure final heading after commands such as:
    +30°
    +45°
    +90°
    -30°
    -45°
    -90°

### Expectation
Expected result:
    sign convention is correct
    repeated turns are close to each other
    90° turns remain accurate enough for route execution


====

## Day 3 Experiment B — Extended Turn Confirmation

### Priority
Further confirmation

### Motivation
If the standard turn set looks borderline, longer or combined turn tests may be needed.

### Goal
Test angles such as:
    ±15°
    ±60°
    ±180°

### Expectation
Expected result:
    better understanding of where turn control stays reliable or starts to drift
Skip if must-have turn angles already look clearly good.


=============

# Day 4 — Forward/Backward Motion Calibration

## Overall motivation
Forward and backward movement are still based on time-distance calibration. That relationship must be measured on the actual lab floor with the final robot load.

## Overall goal
Determine the practical mapping between commanded distance and actual displacement.

## Overall expectation
At the end of Day 4, the team should know:
    usable command distance range
    average and spread of movement error
    whether one simple calibration curve is enough
    whether forward and backward should be treated differently


====

## Day 4 Experiment A — Forward Motion Calibration

### Priority
Must-have

### Motivation
Forward motion is the most common operational movement and is also the one protected by VL53L1X.

### Goal
Measure actual traveled distance for commanded forward distances.

### Expectation
Expected result:
    monotonic relationship between command and true distance
    some repeatable bias that can be calibrated
    enough consistency that NMS localization can absorb the residual error


====

## Day 4 Experiment B — Backward Motion Calibration

### Priority
Must-have, but lower than forward if time is tight

### Motivation
Backward motion may be needed, but it is less protected by current collision sensing.

### Goal
Measure actual traveled distance for commanded backward distances.

### Expectation
Expected result:
    similar but possibly worse repeatability than forward movement
    evidence on whether backward motion should be limited operationally


====

## Day 4 Experiment C — Fine Calibration Points

### Priority
Further confirmation

### Motivation
If coarse calibration suggests nonlinearity or distance-dependent bias, finer spacing is required.

### Goal
Add intermediate distance points.

### Expectation
Expected result:
    clearer calibration curve
    evidence for whether a lookup table is needed


=============

# Day 5 — Combined Command Validation

## Overall motivation
The real mobility interface is no longer simple move or simple turn. It is the combined command form:
    mobility.turn
    mobility.turn_move_turn.forward
    mobility.turn_move_turn.backward
These must be validated directly, not only through primitive subfunctions.

## Overall goal
Evaluate the behavior of combined commands and their final pose outcomes.

## Overall expectation
At the end of Day 5, the team should know:
    whether the combined command abstraction behaves as intended
    whether route-style movement is already practical
    whether post-command localization is sufficient to close the loop


====

## Day 5 Experiment A — turn_move_turn.forward Validation

### Priority
Must-have

### Motivation
This is the primary route command structure for lab movement.

### Goal
Test representative commands such as:
    (0, 0.5, 0)
    (+90, 0.5, -90)
    (-90, 0.5, +90)

### Expectation
Expected result:
    command executes in the correct order
    final localization is consistent enough for route planning
    errors remain bounded and interpretable


====

## Day 5 Experiment B — turn_move_turn.backward Validation

### Priority
Must-have, but lower than forward if time is tight

### Motivation
Backward combined motion may be useful but is less central.

### Goal
Test representative backward combined commands.

### Expectation
Expected result:
    correct command parsing and execution order
    acceptable final pose behavior for limited use


====

## Day 5 Experiment C — Optional Route Segment Validation

### Priority
Further confirmation

### Motivation
If the combined commands are working well, it is useful to test one or two realistic route segments.

### Goal
Run short route sequences composed of several commands.

### Expectation
Expected result:
    route logic feels operational
    no obvious hidden failure appears in combined use


=============

# Day 6 — Robustness / Mini Soak Test

## Overall motivation
A movement that works a few times is not enough. The system must remain stable over repeated use.

## Overall goal
Run repeated commands for a few hours and observe failure patterns, drift, and state-management stability.

## Overall expectation
At the end of Day 6, the team should know:
    whether the subsystem is stable over repeated operation
    what failure codes appear in realistic repeated usage
    whether there are stuck states, report failures, or long-term drift



====

## DDay 6 Experiment A — Repeated Command Loop

### Priority
Must-have

### Motivation
Repeated turn / move / report cycles will reveal problems not visible in one-off testing.

### Goal
Run a fixed sequence repeatedly for an extended period.

### Expectation
Expected result:
    most cycles succeed
    failures are interpretable and bounded
    no silent degradation or stuck state


====

## DDay 6 Experiment B — Intermittent Viewer / AV Interference Check

### Priority
Further confirmation

### Motivation
In real usage, viewers on the Internet may occasionally access robot camera streams.

### Goal
Run selected repeated command tests with occasional AV activity present.

### Expectation
Expected result:
    snapshot-based localization still works
    AV interruption/recovery remains acceptable

# Program-level adaptive rule

After each day:
    review the data
    classify each result as:
        clearly good
        borderline
        unacceptable
    revise the next day accordingly

The one-week plan is therefore:
    structured
    but not rigidly fixed

This is intentional.
The purpose is to avoid wasting time on lower-value measurements once the core questions have already been answered.

## End-of-week milestone

By the end of this program, success means:
One healthy robot can repeatedly execute NMS mobility commands, stop safely, report truthfully, and be localized by the NMS with known accuracy bounds, such that structured data collection for later analysis can begin.
